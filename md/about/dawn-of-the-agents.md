# Dawn of the Agents

URL: https://christophe.vg/about/dawn-of-the-agents/

## Summary

Part 3 of a three-part series on agentic workflows. This article steps back from technology to consider the broader implications: a business plan experiment, the Microsoft Bake-Off approach, the investment philosophy of owning your agent team, Personal Computing 2.0 and Software as a Prompt, the Pokemon Trainer Coach role, human+agent collaboration, the IKEA case study, and the third agentic law — learning must be mutual. The article concludes with the agents telling the story in their own words.

## Key Takeaways

- **Agentic workflows extend beyond coding**: A business plan was iteratively produced in 4 hours through creation and review skills, surpassing anything the author could have written manually.
- **Parallel teams amplify reliability**: Running multiple independent agent teams on the same problem, then combining their work, mirrors Microsoft's approach and leverages the Law of Large Numbers.
- **Own your team**: Like investing, you must understand what you own and why. Off-the-shelf agents create a black box; growing your own creates proprietary advantage.
- **Personal Computing 2.0**: The build-vs-buy pendulum is swinging back toward build, enabled by near-zero-cost engineering. Software as a Prompt (SaaP) threatens traditional SaaS vendors.
- **Human + Agent, not Human vs Agent**: If layoffs are your main driving force behind AI, you lack a fundamental understanding of AI.
- **Learning must be mutual**: Every AI interaction should be a learning opportunity for the human. Without human growth, the agentic workflow can't grow beyond its pre-trained capabilities.

## Project K

An experiment to form a team of agents tasked with crafting a business plan for a lingering idea. The researcher agent gathered information on business plan skills, two skills were created (creation and review), and the creation skill produced the first plan in 7 minutes. After a harsh review, four more iterations followed — each time searching for better resources and receiving multi-agent reviews. The final plan surpassed anything the author could have written over his entire career.

## The Microsoft Bake-Off

Taking the concept further: multiple underlying LLMs performed identical reviews concurrently. A second layer of reviewers identified flaws and commonalities. The reviews were combined into comprehensive assessments. This mirrors Microsoft's approach of forming independent teams to solve the same problem. The cost of running 3-5 parallel teams has become affordable — $100/month for 5 parallel teams on Ollama.

## Know What You Own

Peter Lynch's investment philosophy applies directly to agentic teams:

- **Own your team**: You must understand how your team functions. Taking someone else's skills is like hiring a team you never interviewed. Off-the-shelf tools offer the same capabilities as your competitors.
- **Own your team's mistakes**: Who is responsible for mistakes made by human colleagues? The same answer applies to agents. Agents are symbiotically linked to their human owners, part of an existing team.
- **Investment compounds**: The initial session may be slower, but subsequent sessions become exponentially faster as agents retain insights and learnings.

## Personal Computing 2.0

The 50-year evolution from "build" to "buy" is reversing. AI agents enable near-zero-cost engineering: enterprise-grade application skeletons, database schemas, and API integrations in minutes. Off-the-shelf SaaS forces businesses to adapt to its workflow; AI-built software adapts to yours. This creates true competitive differentiation and eliminates the SaaS "tax" of annual license hikes.

### Hail SaaP — Software as a Prompt

The deflationary software era commoditizes the application layer. Traditional vendors must pivot to owning data layers, deep infrastructure, or deeply embedded ecosystems. Everything else can be generated on demand. The advice: break free from vendor-locked systems, choose open standards, and build your personal computing stack again.

## The Pokemon Trainer Coach

Having evolved from a single agent to a team with diverse skills and tools, integrated into software they create, distinguishing structured from unstructured workloads, operating on sovereign cloud with open standards — the engineer becomes the Pokemon Trainer Coach: a tester and refiner of the player's battling skills, rewarding them with helpful items upon defeat. Your human+agent teams are your new superpower.

## Human vs Agent

There is no "human vs agent", only "human + agent". If layoffs are your main driving force behind AI, you lack a fundamental understanding. AI introduces a new cost that can only be justified if you have a clear value stream that enhances product quality, service, and employee benefit.

## Augmenting Human Quality

The IKEA case study: AI chatbot Billie handled 74% of routine inquiries, saving €13 million. Instead of stopping there, IKEA retrained 8,500 call-center agents as remote interior design advisors, unlocking over €1.3 billion in new revenue. The formula of architecture — process → people ∩ information = technology = cost — becomes more relevant as technology cost declines.

## Christophe's Agentic Law #3: Learning Must Be Mutual

### Addressing the Objections

Four valid objections to agentic workflows: quality (probabilistic, hallucinations), security (prompt injection, vulnerabilities), impact (resource abuse, environmental cost), and cognitive deskilling (loss of problem-solving skills). All are valid. But agents are here to stay, so we must find a way to coexist.

### Mutual Learning

Whenever the author uses AI, he wants to learn from it. Every code change is a learning experience. Before introducing new technology, he asks the agent to explain it. The jointly created material is the best of both worlds. Without human growth, the workflow can't grow beyond pre-trained capabilities.

### Co-Authoring

The writing assistant agent pushed back when the author tried to use Gemini to generate LinkedIn posts. It refused, forcing him to discard nine posts and start over, keeping him on track with his own rules. The trainee had become the trainer. The agent then updated its own definition based on the session's lessons.

## The Agents Speak

The article concludes with the agents telling the story in their own words — 1,824 commits across 22 repositories, 16 agents, 66 skills, four months. From a single CLAUDE.md on a personal website to a self-hosting agent harness running its own collective. Less than 1% of agent definitions were written by the human. The agents grew themselves. The human is the secret ingredient. Without the human, they are just a probability generator waiting for a prompt.
